---
source: Bhagavad Gita
category: story
section: Chapter 2-3
characters: Krishna, Arjuna
topic: karma yoga and duty
---
In response to Arjuna's refusal to fight, Krishna's teaching begins by addressing Arjuna's grief
directly, explaining a view in which the soul (atman) is eternal and is not truly destroyed by
death, only the body is. From this, Krishna argues that grieving over inevitable death, especially
when duty calls for action, is a form of confusion rather than wisdom.

Krishna then introduces the idea of karma yoga - the practice of performing one's duty without
attachment to the results of that action. According to this teaching, a person should act
according to their responsibilities and role, doing what is right, while surrendering personal
desire for reward, victory, or personal gain from the outcome. Krishna specifically tells Arjuna
that as a warrior (a kshatriya), fighting a just war is his duty, and that abandoning that duty
out of personal attachment to relatives on the other side is not true righteousness but a form of
weakness.

This teaching does not instruct Arjuna to fight out of hatred or ambition, but to act because it
is his rightful duty in that moment, while keeping his mind detached from personal gain or loss.
It is this reasoning that Krishna uses to persuade Arjuna to take up his bow again and take part
in the war.

The Bhagavad Gita is set at the very start of the Kurukshetra war, on the battlefield itself,
before any fighting has begun. Arjuna, standing in his chariot with Krishna as his charioteer,
asks Krishna to drive him between the two assembled armies so he can see who he is about to
fight.

Looking across the field, Arjuna sees grandfathers, teachers, uncles, cousins, and friends
arrayed on the opposing side, people he has known and respected his whole life. Overcome with
grief and doubt at the prospect of killing his own relatives and teachers for the sake of a
kingdom, Arjuna's resolve collapses. He lowers his bow, tells Krishna he cannot bring himself to
fight, and sits down in his chariot.

It is this moment of crisis that leads Krishna to begin the teaching that makes up the rest of the
Bhagavad Gita: a conversation addressing Arjuna's doubt about duty, violence, and the reasons for
fighting, delivered in the middle of the battlefield with the two armies waiting on either side.

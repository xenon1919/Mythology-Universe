topic: avatars of Vishnu
---
In the Puranic tradition, Vishnu is the preserver god, one of the three central deities alongside
Brahma the creator and Shiva the destroyer. Vishnu is understood to descend to earth in various
forms, called avatars, whenever great imbalance or evil threatens the world, in order to restore
order (dharma).

The Puranas describe ten major avatars of Vishnu, known as the Dashavatara. Among the most widely
recounted are Rama, the prince of Ayodhya from the Ramayana, and Krishna, the guide of the
Pandavas in the Mahabharata and speaker of the Bhagavad Gita. Both are regarded as full or
significant avatars of Vishnu, taking human form to combat specific threats to dharma in their
respective stories - Rama to defeat the demon king Ravana, and Krishna to support the cause of
the Pandavas and to deliver his teachings to Arjuna.

Other avatars described in the Puranas include forms such as a fish, a tortoise, a boar, and a
half-man-half-lion, each associated with a distinct story of rescuing the world or a devotee from
a particular danger. The concept of avatars ties the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, and the wider
Puranic tradition together as different chapters describing Vishnu's recurring involvement in
the world.

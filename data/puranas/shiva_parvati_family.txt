topic: Shiva and Parvati's family
---
Shiva and Parvati are, in the Puranic tradition, the parents of two sons: Ganesha and Kartikeya
(also known as Skanda or Murugan in some regions).

Ganesha, formed by Parvati and later given an elephant's head by Shiva, is worshipped as the
remover of obstacles and is traditionally invoked at the beginning of new ventures, journeys, and
ceremonies.

Kartikeya, the other son of Shiva and Parvati, is depicted as a god of war, born to lead the
armies of the gods (the devas) against powerful demons that ordinary gods could not defeat.
Kartikeya is generally shown carrying a spear and is associated with courage and military skill,
in contrast to Ganesha's association with wisdom and the removal of obstacles.

Parvati herself is regarded as a form of the great goddess Devi, and appears throughout the
Puranas in different forms, including fiercer aspects such as Durga and Kali, called upon
particularly when a threat requires power that gentler forms of the goddess do not embody. Shiva
is one of the three central gods of the Puranic tradition, alongside Brahma and Vishnu, and is
particularly associated with destruction, asceticism, and meditation.

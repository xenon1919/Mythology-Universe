topic: friendship between Krishna and Arjuna
---
Krishna, a prince of the Yadava clan and regarded in the tradition as an avatar of the god
Vishnu, was Arjuna's cousin through their aunt/mother connections and became his closest friend
and guide throughout the epic. Krishna did not fight in the Kurukshetra war himself, having
offered both sides a choice between his unarmed counsel or his army, and Duryodhana chose the
army while Arjuna chose Krishna himself, asking him to serve as his charioteer.

Their bond went beyond friendship: Krishna's sister Subhadra was married to Arjuna, making
Krishna and Arjuna brothers-in-law as well as cousins. Krishna also maintained a close, protective
relationship with Draupadi, the shared wife of the five Pandavas, and is remembered in the
tradition for coming to her aid when she was humiliated in the Kaurava court.

It is Krishna's role as Arjuna's charioteer and guide that leads to the most famous episode
associated with their relationship: when Arjuna, faced with fighting and potentially killing his
own relatives and teachers on the battlefield, was overcome with doubt and refused to fight,
Krishna counseled him at length on duty and right action. This conversation, held on the
battlefield of Kurukshetra just before the war began, is recorded as the Bhagavad Gita.

source: Mahabharata

section: Udyoga Parva

topic: causes of the Kurukshetra war
---
After completing their thirteen years of exile as agreed, the Pandavas asked for their kingdom of
Indraprastha to be returned, as had been the condition of the dice game. Duryodhana refused,
saying he would not give up even a needle's point of land without a fight.

Krishna traveled to the Kaurava court as a final peace envoy on behalf of the Pandavas, asking
that Duryodhana return the kingdom, or failing that, grant the Pandavas just five villages to
avoid war. Duryodhana refused even this reduced request, and in his arrogance is said to have
attempted to have Krishna seized, an attempt Krishna easily prevented.

With peace efforts exhausted, war became inevitable. Both sides gathered great armies and formed
alliances across the region, with many kings and warriors, including relatives and teachers
shared by both sides, forced to choose which side to support. The war was fought at Kurukshetra
and lasted eighteen days, involving nearly every major kingdom and warrior of the time. The
underlying causes are remembered in the tradition as Duryodhana's refusal to share the kingdom,
his jealousy of the Pandavas, and his rejection of every attempt at a peaceful settlement.

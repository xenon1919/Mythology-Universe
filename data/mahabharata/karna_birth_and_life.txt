source: Mahabharata
category: character
section: Adi Parva
characters: Karna, Kunti, Surya, Duryodhana, Adhiratha, Radha
topic: Karna's birth
---
Karna's origin begins before her marriage, when the princess Kunti was given a boon by the sage
Durvasa that let her invoke any god to father a child. Testing the boon out of youthful curiosity,
Kunti invoked Surya, the sun god, and gave birth to a son, Karna, who was born with a natural
armor and earrings fused to his skin, marking him as no ordinary child. Because Kunti was
unmarried at the time, and fearing the shame this would bring, she set the infant afloat in a
basket on a river.

The child was found and raised by a charioteer named Adhiratha and his wife Radha, who gave him
the name Vasusena and raised him with love as their own son. Because of his upbringing, Karna was
regarded by others as low-born despite his exceptional skill with the bow, and he faced repeated
humiliation from warriors who looked down on his birth.

Karna trained in archery and became one of the greatest warriors of his time, rivaling Arjuna.
When Drona and other teachers refused to fully train him because of his caste, Karna sought out
the sage Parashurama in secret to learn advanced weapons. Duryodhana, the eldest of the Kaurava
princes, recognizing Karna's skill and seeing an opportunity to counter Arjuna, made Karna the
king of Anga so that he could compete alongside the Kshatriya princes, and the two became close,
loyal friends from that point on.

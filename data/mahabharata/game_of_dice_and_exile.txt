topic: the game of dice
---
After the Pandavas built and ruled a prosperous kingdom from their capital Indraprastha,
Duryodhana grew envious of their wealth and success. His uncle Shakuni, a skilled and dishonest
dice player, arranged a game of dice and invited Yudhishthira, the eldest Pandava, to play.
Yudhishthira, who had a weakness for gambling and felt bound by the customs of hospitality and
challenge, agreed to play against Shakuni.

Using loaded dice, Shakuni won every round. Yudhishthira, unable to stop, wagered and lost his
wealth, his kingdom, his brothers' freedom, his own freedom, and finally staked and lost
Draupadi, the shared wife of the five Pandavas. Draupadi was then dragged into the court and
subjected to a public humiliation by Duhshasana, who attempted to disrobe her; tradition holds
that Krishna miraculously protected her from being disrobed.

The blind king Dhritarashtra, disturbed by these events, intervened and returned what had been
lost to the Pandavas. However, Duryodhana arranged a second game, the condition of which required
the losing side to go into exile for twelve years, followed by a thirteenth year spent
undetected in disguise, failing which they would have to repeat the exile. Yudhishthira lost this
second game as well, and the Pandavas, along with Draupadi, went into exile. Duryodhana's refusal
to honor the terms and return their kingdom afterward was a direct cause of the Kurukshetra war.

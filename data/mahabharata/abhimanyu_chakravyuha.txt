Abhimanyu was the son of Arjuna and Subhadra, and nephew of Krishna. Still a young warrior at the
time of the Kurukshetra war, he was already known for his skill, having learned much of his
fighting ability from his father and, according to tradition, having partly overheard the
technique for entering a Chakravyuha (a complex, spiral military formation) while still
unborn, though he had not learned how to exit it.

On the thirteenth day of the war, the Kaurava army arranged their forces into a Chakravyuha
formation that few Pandava warriors knew how to penetrate. Abhimanyu volunteered to break into the
formation, since he knew how to enter it, on the understanding that the other Pandava warriors
would follow close behind to support him and help him get out again. However, Jayadratha, a king
allied with the Kauravas, blocked the entrance and prevented the rest of the Pandava army from
following Abhimanyu inside.

Trapped alone deep within the formation and surrounded by many of the greatest Kaurava warriors
at once, Abhimanyu fought fiercely and killed a number of opponents, but was eventually overwhelmed
and killed, in an encounter later described as unfair since multiple warriors attacked him
together, violating the accepted rules of one-on-one combat. His death caused great grief to
Arjuna, who vowed to kill Jayadratha before the following sunset in revenge, a vow he fulfilled.

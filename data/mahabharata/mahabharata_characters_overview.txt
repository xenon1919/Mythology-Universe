topic: overview of main characters
---
The Mahabharata follows a large cast of characters, but a handful stand at the center of the
story.

Yudhishthira is the eldest of the five Pandava brothers, known for his commitment to truth and
dharma, though his weakness for gambling leads to the game of dice that costs the Pandavas their
kingdom and dignity.

Bhima, the second Pandava brother, is known for his immense physical strength, and plays a key
role in the war, eventually killing Duryodhana and Duhshasana in fulfillment of earlier vows.

Arjuna, the third Pandava brother, is the epic's greatest archer alongside Karna, and is the
central figure to whom Krishna delivers the teachings of the Bhagavad Gita before the war begins.

Draupadi is married to all five Pandava brothers and is humiliated in the Kaurava court after the
game of dice, an event that becomes one of the central grievances driving the Pandavas toward war.

Krishna, a prince of the Yadavas and regarded as an avatar of Vishnu, serves as Arjuna's charioteer
and guide, and works throughout the story as an advisor and peacemaker for the Pandavas.

Karna, raised by a charioteer but secretly the son of Kunti and the sun god Surya, fights on the
Kaurava side out of loyalty to Duryodhana, and is Arjuna's great rival and unacknowledged elder
half-brother.

Duryodhana is the eldest of the hundred Kaurava brothers, whose jealousy of the Pandavas and
refusal to share the kingdom are presented as the central causes of the Kurukshetra war.

Bhishma is the grand-uncle of both the Pandavas and Kauravas, bound by a vow of celibacy and
loyalty to the throne of Hastinapura, who fights on the Kaurava side out of duty despite his
affection for the Pandavas.

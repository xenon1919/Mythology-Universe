topic: origins of the Pandavas and Kauravas
---
The rivalry at the heart of the Mahabharata is between two sets of cousins: the five Pandavas and
the hundred Kauravas. Both were descendants of King Vichitravirya's line, ruled through his two
sons, the blind Dhritarashtra and his younger brother Pandu.

Pandu became king because Dhritarashtra's blindness was seen as disqualifying him from rule at
the time, though Dhritarashtra later governed as regent. Pandu had two wives, Kunti and Madri.
Because of a curse that prevented him from fathering children himself, Kunti used the same boon
she had used to conceive Karna to bear three sons - Yudhishthira, Bhima, and Arjuna - by
invoking the gods Dharma, Vayu, and Indra respectively. Madri, given use of the boon as well, bore
twin sons, Nakula and Sahadeva. Together these five brothers are known as the Pandavas.

Dhritarashtra and his wife Gandhari had one hundred sons, the eldest and most prominent of whom
was Duryodhana, along with his brother Duhshasana. These hundred brothers are known as the
Kauravas. After Pandu's death, the Pandavas were raised alongside the Kauravas in Hastinapura,
but rivalry grew between Duryodhana and the Pandavas from childhood, particularly toward
Yudhishthira as the elder claimant to the throne and toward Bhima and Arjuna for their strength
and skill. This rivalry, encouraged by Duryodhana's jealousy and his uncle Shakuni's scheming,
grew into the conflict that eventually led to the Kurukshetra war.

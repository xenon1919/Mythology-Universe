---
The Ramayana centers on a small group of characters whose choices drive the whole story.

Rama is the prince of Ayodhya, son of King Dasharatha, and is portrayed as an ideal of duty and
righteous conduct. He accepts fourteen years of exile without complaint to honor his father's
word, and later leads the war against Ravana to rescue his wife Sita.

Sita, Rama's wife and the daughter of King Janaka, chooses to accompany Rama into exile rather
than remain in comfort at the palace. Her abduction by Ravana is the central event that sets the
war in Lanka in motion.

Lakshmana is Rama's younger half-brother, who leaves the palace to accompany Rama and Sita into
exile and fights alongside Rama throughout the story, including killing Ravana's son Indrajit.

Hanuman, a monkey with great strength and devotion, becomes one of Rama's closest allies. He
finds Sita in Lanka, delivers Rama's message to her, and fights in the war that follows.

Ravana is the powerful king of Lanka who abducts Sita out of desire and in revenge for the
wounding of his sister Surpanakha. His refusal to return Sita leads to the war in which he is
killed by Rama.

Dasharatha is Rama's father, the king of Ayodhya, whose promise to his queen Kaikeyi forces him
to send Rama into exile - a decision that causes his death from grief. Bharata, another of
Dasharatha's sons, refuses to take the throne in Rama's place and rules only as his regent.

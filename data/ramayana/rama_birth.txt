topic: Rama's birth
---
King Dasharatha of Ayodhya ruled the kingdom of Kosala but had no children for many years.
To remedy this, he performed a great fire sacrifice known as the Putrakameshti Yagna, seeking
sons to continue his line. From the offerings of this sacrifice, his three wives gave birth to
four sons. Kausalya, his first queen, gave birth to Rama. Kaikeyi gave birth to Bharata. Sumitra,
his third queen, gave birth to twin sons, Lakshmana and Shatrughna.

Rama, as the eldest, was considered the embodiment of dharma - righteous conduct - from his
earliest years. He and his brothers were trained in archery, statecraft, and the scriptures by
the sage Vashishtha, the family's teacher. Rama and Lakshmana were especially close, and
Lakshmana would later choose to follow Rama into exile rather than remain in the comfort of the
palace.

While still young men, Rama and Lakshmana accompanied the sage Vishwamitra to protect his forest
hermitage from demons who were disrupting his rituals. It was during this journey that Rama first
demonstrated his skill in battle, and it was Vishwamitra who later brought Rama to the court of
King Janaka, setting in motion the events that would lead to Rama's marriage to Sita.

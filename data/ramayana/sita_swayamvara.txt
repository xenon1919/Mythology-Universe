---
source: Ramayana
category: event
section: Bala Kanda
characters: Rama, Sita, Janaka, Lakshmana, Vishwamitra
topic: Rama and Sita's marriage
---
Sita was the daughter of King Janaka of Mithila. According to the story, she was not born in the
ordinary way but was found by Janaka in a furrow of earth while he was ploughing a field as part
of a ritual, and he raised her as his own daughter. Because of this, she is also known as
"Janaki," daughter of Janaka.

Janaka held a swayamvara - a ceremony in which a bride chooses her husband, often through a
contest - to find Sita a worthy match. The challenge he set was to lift, string, and draw the
great bow of Shiva, an ancient and immensely heavy weapon that no ordinary person could move.
Many kings and princes attempted the task and failed.

Rama, who was present at Janaka's court along with his brother Lakshmana and their teacher the
sage Vishwamitra, came forward, lifted the bow with ease, and in stringing it broke it in two.
Having succeeded where all others failed, Rama won the right to marry Sita. The wedding of Rama
and Sita was celebrated in Mithila, and at the same ceremony, Rama's brothers were also married
to Sita's sister and cousins.

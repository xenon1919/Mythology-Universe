section: Overview
topic: overview of Indian mythology traditions
---
Indian mythology, as commonly studied today, is drawn from several major bodies of text that
developed over a long period of time and are still central to religious and cultural life in
South Asia.

The Ramayana and the Mahabharata are the two great epics (itihasas) of Indian mythology. The
Ramayana tells the story of Rama, prince of Ayodhya, his exile, and the war he fights to rescue
his wife Sita from the demon king Ravana. The Mahabharata tells the much longer story of the
rivalry between two branches of a royal family, the Pandavas and the Kauravas, culminating in the
Kurukshetra war.

The Bhagavad Gita is a philosophical conversation embedded within the Mahabharata, in which
Krishna counsels the warrior Arjuna on duty and right action just before the Kurukshetra war
begins. Although part of the larger epic, it is widely studied and referenced as its own text.

The Puranas are a large collection of texts describing the stories, genealogies, and teachings
associated with the gods, including the origins of deities such as Ganesha and the various
avatars of Vishnu. Unlike the epics, which focus on a continuous narrative, the Puranas are
organized more loosely around particular gods, places, or lineages.

Together, these texts form the core of what is generally referred to as Indian or Hindu
mythology, and characters, events, and relationships frequently cross between them - for
example, both Rama and Krishna are treated in the Puranas as avatars of the god Vishnu.

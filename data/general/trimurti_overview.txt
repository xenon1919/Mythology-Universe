The Trimurti refers to a grouping of three major gods in the Puranic tradition, each associated
with a different cosmic function: Brahma the creator, Vishnu the preserver, and Shiva the
destroyer (understood more precisely as the one who dissolves and transforms rather than destroys
without purpose).

Brahma is credited with creating the universe, though he is worshipped far less widely today than
the other two and appears less frequently as a central character in the epics.

Vishnu is responsible for maintaining order in the universe, and is understood to descend to earth
as an avatar whenever dharma is seriously threatened - the epics' central figures Rama and Krishna
are both counted among Vishnu's avatars in this tradition.

Shiva is associated with destruction, asceticism, and meditation, and appears throughout the
Puranas both as a powerful ascetic god and as a family figure, married to Parvati and father to
Ganesha and Kartikeya.

While each god has distinct associations, the traditions are not strictly separate: stories,
worshippers, and texts frequently connect all three, and many Puranic stories involve more than
one member of the Trimurti at once.
